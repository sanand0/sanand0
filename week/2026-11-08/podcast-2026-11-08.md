Alex: A big body of private research this week asked a similarly operational question: can you redirect an agent while it is still working, rather than wait for the wrong answer?
Maya: The answer is “sometimes, in the visible interface,” not “the model instantly forgets everything.” Three valid trials covered Claude, ChatGPT Chat, and ChatGPT Work. The correction arrived before the original long task finished in each one.
Alex: The measurements were UI events and final transcripts, not hidden reasoning or billable tokens. One ChatGPT trial even showed an intermediate completion signal too early, so the final transcript had to settle what happened.
Maya: And the controls can mislead you. Typing a correction can replace the Stop button with Send while the old response is still running. So button disappearance alone is not proof that the task ended.
Alex: That is the kind of mechanism I like: a visible button state is a poor proxy for backend state. The practical guide says interrupt when the next few minutes would be wasted or risky, but queue a cosmetic change.
Maya: Those time estimates are explicitly rules of thumb, not measured break-even points. No one measured whether unfinished reasoning is reused or whether an interruption saves tokens. The guide narrows its claim to what the browser showed.
